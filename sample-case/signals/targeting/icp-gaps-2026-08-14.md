## Finding 1 — no contractor publishes a technician count. Not one.

Zero of eight. Not a single company in the segment publishes the number the ICP is written in.
Every row in today's CSV is sized by a **proxy**, named per row:

| Proxy | Used for | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Fleet / vehicle count, first-party | 128 Plumbing (120), Crest (40+), Rowell's (23 itemised) | **best available** — a truck is roughly a tech |
| Total employee count, first-party | Parham (50+), Rowell's (73) | good, but includes office staff |
| Branch-location count, first-party | Accurate (6), Mechanical One (9 metros) | structural, not numeric |
| Revenue estimate, third-party vendor | Genz-Ryan ($56.4M), Burkholder's ($17.2M) | **weak** — vendor guess, not a filing |
| Employee bucket, third-party | Accurate (51–200), Burkholder's (51–200) | weakest — the bucket is wider than the ICP |

**Therefore:** delete "20–200 technicians" from `CLAUDE.md` §1 and replace it with the proxy the AI
SDR can actually filter on: **a fleet of ~25–150 service vehicles, or 50–250 total employees.**
Keep the tech count as a call-one qualifying question, not a targeting filter.

## Finding 2 — "owner-operated" is actively contradicted by the companies' own websites.

This is the headline. **Five of the ten candidates I dropped were not owner-operated — and every
one of the five describes itself as family-owned or employee-owned in its own materials.**

| Company | Says on its own site | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| Western Heating & Air (Boise, ID) | "family-owned… third-generation company with over 200 employees" | **The SEER Group took a majority stake, July 2021** |
| Bell Brothers (Sacramento, CA) | "family owned and operated," 140 trucks / 200 employees | **Service Champions / CenterOak Partners (PE), Feb 2020** |
| James Lane A/C & Plumbing (Wichita Falls, TX) | "family-owned," ACCA named it family-owned in **2025** | **"A Fidelity Building Services Group Company"** |
| Deluxe Plumbing & Heating (Bethlehem, PA) | PHCC named Larry Shoemaker **"Owner"** in 2025 | **Acquired by HomeX Services Group** (their 16th) |
| Action Air Systems (Manchester, CT) | "we employ over 50 people" | **ESOP — employee-owned; no owner/CFO buyer exists** |

Two of those five were *certified as family-owned by an industry association in 2025* while under
outside ownership. An award is not an ownership record.

**Therefore:** "owner-operated" stays in the ICP — it is the right criterion — but it may **never**
be sourced from the company's About page. It must be checked against an ownership record. Sourceable
options found today, in order of usefulness:

- **PE roll-up trackers** — PrivSource, CT Acquisitions, DealSeam all publish HVAC/plumbing
  acquisition trackers naming platforms and their portfolio companies. Screen every candidate
  against these before it enters the CSV.
- **`whoownsyourhvac.org`** — a database of 93 verified independent Colorado contractors, built
  from "corporate filings, PE portfolio pages, and community tips," and it separately flags
  **30 PE-backed brands in Colorado that still market themselves as local.** Colorado-only, but it
  is exactly the artifact this ICP needs, and it proves the check is doable.
- **A positive independence signal** — the strongest row on today's list, 128 Plumbing, is strong
  precisely because it published a press release *stating* it will not sell (Certified Evergreen).
  Contractors who reject PE tend to say so loudly. That is a searchable, high-precision signal.

## Finding 3 — the exclusion you added cannot be fully enforced.

"Locally-owned franchisees of national brands" is a real and correct exclusion. I screened all 18
candidates against known franchise brands (Mr. Rooter, Roto-Rooter, One Hour, Benjamin Franklin,
Aire Serv, Milestone) and checked site footers for franchise disclosures. **None of the 8 appears to
be a franchisee, and Parham's About page states outright that it is not a franchise.**

But a franchisee that operates under its own name with no brand disclosure is **indistinguishable
from an independent** in public data. I could not falsify this for the other 7. Treat the exclusion
as applied-best-effort, not as verified.

## Finding 4 — size proxies contradict each other, and sometimes themselves.

Worth knowing before anyone trusts a headcount field:

- **Crest Mechanical's own About page** gives "approximately 80 dedicated employees" and
  "over 100 employees" — on the same page.
- **Burkholder's** has three third-party numbers: 18 people, the 51–200 bucket, and $17.2M revenue.
  The first is almost certainly a contact-record count, not headcount. No first-party figure exists.
- **Western Heating**: SEER's 2021 release said "nearly one hundred employees"; the company site now
  says "over 200." Could be five years of growth, could be marketing. Unresolvable from outside.
- **Peterman Brothers**: one vendor says 183 employees, another says 500+. Dropped for this reason
  as much as for size.
- **Mechanical One** publishes no headcount but claims "thousands of jobs created," against a vendor
  figure of 67 employees.

**Therefore:** never carry a single headcount number without its source. The CSV's `headcount`
column names the proxy for every row, per instruction.

## Also learned about the segment itself

- **The 20-tech floor kills more candidates than the 200 ceiling.** Three drops were too small
  (Cardinal Plumbing 15 employees, McKendree's 25, PV Heating ~$5M revenue). The "under 10 techs"
  exclusion in §1 is set too low to be useful — the real floor in practice is nearer 40 total
  employees. Consider raising it.
- **The segment is consolidating fast, which is why Finding 2 keeps happening.** Platform-level
  deals in the trailing 18 months alone: Sila→Goldman Sachs Alternatives, Apex $3.4B continuation,
  Service Logic→Bain/Mubadala, Redwood→Altas, Champions Group→Blackstone. Any lead list older than
  a few months is decaying — a company that qualified in Q1 may not qualify now. **Re-verify
  ownership before contact, not just before scoring.**
- **The buyer titles in §1 are right, and better than expected.** Owner/CFO/co-owner all appear
  publicly. Two rows produced an exact hit — Accurate's Michael Linton is "Co-Owner, CFO" in one
  person; Crest lists both a President/CEO and a CFO, both family. Where the title is "President"
  of a 2nd/3rd-generation family firm, treat it as owner-equivalent but say so.
- **Trade press and association awards are the highest-yield discovery channel** for this segment
  (ACHR News, PHCC, ACCA, Pumper, Inc. 5000, regional business journals) — they publish headcount
  and name the owner in the same article. **But they are not an ownership check** (see Finding 2).
  Inc. 5000 is the exception: its listing requirements independently verify a company is privately
  held and independent.
