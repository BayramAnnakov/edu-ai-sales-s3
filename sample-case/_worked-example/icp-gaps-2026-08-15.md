# ICP gaps — 2026-08-15

**Query run:** US field-service contractors (HVAC · plumbing · mechanical), 20–200 technicians,
owner-operated; buyer = owner / CFO / co-owner. Excluding national franchises and shops under
10 techs.
**Searched with:** WebSearch + WebFetch — **no LinkedIn/Crunchbase/company-data MCP was connected**,
so no headcount database, no title filter, no employee-count field was available at any point.
**Candidates seen:** 14 · **Survived verification:** 5

| Criterion | Sourced for | Proxy used | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade = HVAC / plumbing / mechanical field service | 5 of 5 | — (stated directly on every company site) | **delete** |
| US geography | 5 of 5 | — | **delete** |
| **20–200 technicians** | **0 of 5** | total employee count on the company's own About page (2 of 5); branch/location count (2 of 5); nothing at all (1 of 5) | **proxy** |
| **Owner-operated** | 5 of 5 (but see below) | company self-description + negative screen against PE roll-up trackers | **proxy** |
| Not a national franchise | 5 of 5 | — (no candidate was a franchisee) | **delete** |
| Buyer is owner / CFO / co-owner | 5 of 5 | named leadership page on the company's own site | keep |
| **ACV ~$22,000 fit** | **0 of 5** | none found | **call** |

**Therefore, per criterion:**

- **20–200 technicians → proxy.** No company database publishes a technician count, and none was
  reachable here anyway. The best available stand-in is the **total employee count a contractor
  publishes on its own About page**. What it gets wrong: it counts dispatchers, estimators, office
  and warehouse staff alongside field techs. Kennedy Mechanical's "over 100 employees" and James
  Lane's "~70 employees" could each be 40 techs or 85. **Two of five leads publish no number at
  all** — for those, the only proxy is branch count, which is weaker still: a four-branch
  residential plumber can run 15 techs and fall under the ICP floor entirely.
  - **Registry check, and it failed:** I looked for a licensing route. State contractor/trade
    licence boards (NC's plumbing & HVAC board, Texas TDLR and TSBPE, WV Division of Labor) do
    publish per-company licence records — but a licence record carries the *licence holder*, not a
    staff count. **`PROXY UNVERIFIED` for headcount purposes: the registries exist and are reachable,
    but they do not carry the field.** Ruled out for the same reason: a carrier census (power units
    / driver counts) binds above a weight threshold that HVAC and plumbing service vans sit below,
    so contractors of this size largely do not appear.
  - **What would actually work and was not run:** job postings. A contractor hiring three service
    techs and a dispatcher is describing its own crew size and its own pain in its own words. That
    is the highest-fidelity free source for this criterion and it is worth a dedicated pass.

- **Owner-operated → proxy, and the proxy earned its place.** Company self-description alone is
  not enough — sites go stale after an acquisition. The **PE roll-up trackers published by M&A
  advisers** (which name active platforms: Apex Service Partners, Wrench Group, Sila, ARS, TurnPoint,
  Service Experts, Comfort Systems) work as a **negative screen**. This screen removed a real
  candidate: **Benoure Plumbing, Heating & AC** (South Burlington VT) publishes "over 85 employees,
  over 70 licensed plumbers and HVAC technicians in the field" — the single best headcount match
  found all day — but was **acquired by Empowered Ventures in April 2024** and is now a holding-company
  ESOP, not owner-operated. Its own site still reads as independent.
  What the proxy gets wrong: **absence from a tracker is not evidence of independence.** These lists
  cover announced deals by named platforms. A quiet local sale, a search fund, or a minority
  investment leaves no trace. High precision, low recall — everyone it flags is genuinely not
  owner-operated; plenty who aren't owner-operated go unflagged.

- **ACV ~$22,000 fit → call.** No public record exists of a private contractor's software budget.
  This is not a targeting criterion, it is the **first qualification question on the call**. It
  should be moved out of section 1 of `CLAUDE.md` and into the call, or it will keep looking like a
  filter while filtering nothing.

- **"Not a national franchise" → delete.** It excluded zero candidates. Every company that surfaced
  through this search route was independent, because the search route itself ("family owned",
  "since 19xx", trade-association awards) selects for independents. The filter did no work here.
  **It may still do work under a different search route** — a local-listings or reviews-based
  search would surface franchisees — so this is "delete from the scoring rubric", not "delete the
  concern".

- **Trade and US geography → delete.** Both passed 5 of 5. A criterion that passes everything it
  touches is identical to not having it. They define the search string; they are not scoring
  criteria and should not carry points.

**Also learned:**

1. **The searchable segment is not the segment in the contract.** The companies I could evidence are
   overwhelmingly **commercial mechanical contractors** (Kennedy: plumbing + HVAC + fire protection;
   Kahn: building automation, commercial kitchen, refrigeration; Accurate: design-build, data centre
   HVAC). The contract says "field-service contractors… dispatch, scheduling, work orders, tech
   mobile app," which reads residential/light-commercial. **These are two different buyers with two
   different pains,** and the search kept returning the first one. Worth deciding which you actually
   sell to before the next run.
2. **Trade-association award coverage is the highest-yield free source for this ICP.** ACCA's annual
   awards and the trade press covering them (ACHR News, Plumbing & Mechanical) name the company, the
   city, a named owner, and often the size and location count — in one article. Two of the five
   leads came from a single ACCA awards page. It is a small, high-precision list.
3. **A published headcount is rarer than a published owner name.** Every one of the five names a
   leader on its own site; only two publish any staff number. If headcount is the binding constraint
   in the ICP, the current search route cannot enforce it.
4. **Two candidates were dropped on size, not on evidence:** Arden Engineering Constructors
   (Pawtucket RI) publishes 230+ tradespeople — over the 200 ceiling; and Murphy Company is far
   larger still. The ceiling is doing real work at the top end even though the floor cannot be
   enforced at the bottom.
5. **Three company sites (McLay Services, Elander Mechanical, Medford Wellington) returned 403 to
   automated fetching.** They are not disqualified — they are unverifiable by this method. A human
   opening them in a browser would resolve all three in about four minutes.
