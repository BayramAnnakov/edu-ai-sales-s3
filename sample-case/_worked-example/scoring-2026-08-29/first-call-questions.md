# First call — the questions

**Last updated:** 2026-08-29
**Why this page exists:** everything below is something no database will tell you. Each one was a
targeting or scoring criterion that could not be sourced from outside, so it stopped being a filter
and became a question. Six questions, in the order you would actually ask them.

> Ask these in a conversation, not as a form. If you get through four of the six, the call worked.

---

### 1 · "What do you pay for the system you're on now — all in, per year?"

*What it settles:* whether they can carry ~$22,000/year (the ACV fit criterion).
*Why you have to ask:* the 15 Aug sourcing run resolved this for **0 of 5** companies. No public
record exists of a private contractor's software budget, and no proxy was found — not one.
*Ask it this way,* not **"What's your budget for this?"** — nobody has a budget for a thing they
have not decided to buy, and the honest answer to that question is "I don't know", which teaches you
nothing. What they already pay is a number they know, and it is the anchor.

---

### 2 · "How many of your people carry a phone into a customer's building on a Tuesday?"

*What it settles:* the 20–200 technician band (F1) — the criterion the whole ICP is built on.
*Why you have to ask:* **0 of 5** companies published a technician count. Two published a total
employee count that includes dispatchers, estimators, office and warehouse staff; two published only
a branch count; one published nothing at all. "Over 100 employees" could be 40 techs or 85.
*Ask it this way,* not **"How many employees do you have?"** — that is the number that is already
wrong on their website, and asking it gets you the same wrong number out loud.

---

### 3 · "Who owns the company today?"

*What it settles:* owner-operated (F2), and whether the buyer you are talking to can actually decide.
*Why you have to ask:* company sites go stale after an acquisition. The Aug-15 run caught
**Benoure Plumbing, Heating & AC** — best headcount match found all day, still reading as
independent on its own About page, and acquired in April 2024. The PE roll-up screen has high
precision and **low recall**: everyone it flags is genuinely not independent, and a quiet local sale
or a search fund leaves no trace at all.
*Ask it this way,* not **"You're family-owned, right?"** — a leading question about a fact that is
frequently and innocently misstated on the company's own website gets you a confirmation, not a fact.

---

### 4 · "What do you run your books in, and what has to flow into it?"

*What it settles:* the integration condition — a pass/fail requirement, not an intent signal.
*Why you have to ask:* **this is the question that lost Northline Mechanical.** Lisa Grant named it
on call **1** — *"if it doesn't talk to QuickBooks Enterprise, it's a non-starter"* — the rep did not
know the answer, it was never followed up, and the deal ran nine more calls and died. The CRM
recorded the loss reason as "went quiet".
*Ask it this way,* not **"Do you use QuickBooks?"** — the version that matters (Enterprise vs Online
vs Desktop) and the direction of flow are the whole answer, and yes/no hides both.
🔴 **If you do not know whether we support what they name, say so on the call and give a date.**
A paragraph about our platform's flexibility tells them the answer is no.

---

### 5 · "Walk me through your last job — who booked it, who scheduled it, who closed it out."

*What it settles:* commercial vs residential/light-commercial — the unresolved ICP question.
*Why you have to ask:* the Aug-15 run found that the segment ServiceGrid can *find* is commercial
mechanical contractors, while `CLAUDE.md` §1 describes a residential/light-commercial motion. Two
different buyers, two different pains. Nobody has decided which one we sell to, so the call has to
find out one lead at a time.
*Ask it this way,* not **"Are you commercial or residential?"** — most contractors answer "both",
which is true and useless. The shape of one real job tells you which one pays the bills.

---

### 6 · "How did you choose the system you're on now?"

*What it settles:* who else has to say yes, and how long that took last time.
*Why you have to ask:* no proxy exists for a private company's decision process, and it is the
thing that turns a 45-day cycle into a 120-day one.
*Ask it this way,* not **"Who's the decision maker?"** — that question makes the person in front of
you defend their own authority, and the answer is always "me". The history of the last decision is
the truth about the next one.

---

## Also worth asking, and it is not a qualification question

### "What decision are you trying to make that you can't make today?"

Not on this list because it settles no criterion. It is here because Lisa Grant answered it
**three times unprompted** — job-level profitability, for her board — and was talked over each time.
The one thing the only lost-deal record in this repo actually proves is that ServiceGrid's calls
do not leave room for the answer.
